The idea in plain words

In reinforcement learning an agent acts in an environment: it sees a state st, picks an action at from its policy π(a | s), and gets a reward rt. It learns by trial and error to maximize the total future reward.

Policy gradient pushes up the log-probability of actions that turned out better than expected and pushes down the others: maximize E[log πθ(at | st)·Ât]. "Better than expected" is the advantage Ât = Gt − V(st): the actual discounted return minus what the critic network predicted for that state.

Big policy updates can wreck a policy that was working. Trust-region methods limit the change with a KL-divergence constraint, which is complex to optimize. PPO gets the same effect simply: it clips the probability ratio πnew/πold to [1 − ε, 1 + ε], so one update can't move the policy far. An entropy bonus keeps the policy exploring.

Formula card

PPO
  • Discounted return: Gt = rt + γrt+1 + γ²rt+2 + … ; compute backwards: Gt = rt + γGt+1. γ ∈ [0, 1]: 0 = only the immediate reward, 1 = all future rewards count equally.
  • Advantage: Ât = Gt − V(st). Positive: the action was better than average.
  • Critic loss: (Gt − V(st))², so the critic learns to predict the average return of a state.
  • Ratio: rt(θ) = πθ(at | st) / πold(at | st) = exp(log πnew − log πold).
  • Clipped objective: L = E[min(rtÂt, clip(rt, 1 − ε, 1 + ε)Ât)], ε ≈ 0.2. Actor loss = −L − λ·entropy.
  • Bigger entropy coefficient λ → more exploration (more random policy); smaller → more exploitation.

Answer and solution
Answer: [2.71, 1.9, 1]
  1. Work backwards with Gt = rt + γ·Gt+1 (G after the last step is 0).
  2. G2 = 1 = 1
  3. G1 = 1 + 0.9 × 1 = 1.9
  4. G0 = 1 + 0.9 × 1.9 = 2.71

C. Workshop questions (1 point each)

C1Warm-up1 pt
What is reinforcement learning?
Answer and solution
Answer: Learning by trial and error: an agent acts in an environment and learns a policy that maximizes cumulative reward.

Workshop Q1.

C2Warm-up1 pt
Define agent, environment, policy, state, action and reward.
Answer and solution
Answer: Agent: the learner/actor. Environment: what it interacts with. Policy π(a | s): probability of each action in a state. State: a snapshot of the environment. Action: what the agent does. Reward: the feedback number after an action.

Workshop Q2.

C3Warm-up1 pt
What is Gt and how do we get it?
Answer and solution
Answer: The discounted sum of rewards from t to the end of the episode; collect an episode, then go backwards in time computing rt + γGt+1.

Workshop Q3.

C4Warm-up1 pt
How does the critic network relate to Gt?
Answer and solution
Answer: It is trained to predict Gt from the state, minimizing (Gt − V(s))², so V(s) becomes the average return from that state.

Workshop Q4.

C5Warm-up1 pt
What is γ, its range, and what do 0 and 1 mean?
Answer and solution
Answer: The discount factor in [0, 1]: 0 = only the immediate reward matters; 1 = all future rewards count equally.

Workshop Q5.

C6Warm-up1 pt
What is V(s), and how do we obtain it?
Answer and solution
Answer: The state value: the expected discounted return starting from s; it's the critic network's output.

Workshop Q6.

C7Warm-up1 pt
What does the advantage Ât = Gt − V(s) really mean?
Answer and solution
Answer: How much better (positive) or worse (negative) the action turned out than the average outcome from that state.

Workshop Q7.

C8Warm-up1 pt
How do we get log π(at | st), and what is its size?
Answer and solution
Answer: The actor network outputs a distribution over actions; take the log-probability of the chosen action: one number per time step.

Workshop Q8.

C9Warm-up1 pt
What does E[log πθ(at | st)Ât] mean?
Answer and solution
Answer: Increase the probability of actions with positive advantage and decrease those with negative advantage, weighted by how much better or worse they were.

Workshop Q9.

C10Warm-up1 pt
What are the probability ratio and the KL divergence for in trust-region methods?
Answer and solution
Answer: The ratio measures how much the new policy changed an action's probability; the KL constraint keeps the new policy close to the old one so updates stay stable.

Workshop Q10.

C11Warm-up1 pt
What is the limitation of trust-region methods?
Answer and solution
Answer: The KL-constrained optimization is complicated and expensive (second-order methods).

Workshop Q11.

C12Warm-up1 pt
How does PPO's clipping fix it?
Answer and solution
Answer: It replaces the constraint with a simple clipped objective: ratios outside [1 − ε, 1 + ε] give no extra benefit, so ordinary gradient descent keeps updates small.

Workshop Q12.

C13Warm-up1 pt
What is the entropy term in PPO? A bigger or smaller coefficient?
Answer and solution
Answer: A bonus for policy randomness that encourages exploration: bigger → more exploration; smaller → the policy commits sooner to what it already believes.
